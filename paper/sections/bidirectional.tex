No statistic computed from draws of $q_\theta$ alone can detect an unvisited basin (Proposition~\ref{prop:unvisited}). Breaking this blindness requires configurations that $q_\theta$ did not propose. Suppose an independent reference sampler, such as parallel tempering or annealed importance sampling, provides $y_1,\dots,y_K$ approximately distributed according to $p$. Writing $\ell(x)=-\beta U(x)-\log q_\theta(x)$ for the unnormalized log importance weight, two diagnostic gaps arise from Jensen's inequality:
\begin{equation}
G_{\rm fwd}=\log\hat Z_M-\frac1M\sum_{k=1}^M\ell(x_k),\qquad
G_{\rm rev}=\frac1K\sum_{j=1}^K\ell(y_j)-\log\hat Z_M.
\end{equation}
By Jensen's inequality applied to the concave logarithm, $\log(\frac1M\sum_k e^{\ell(x_k)})\ge\frac1M\sum_k\ell(x_k)$, so $G_{\rm fwd}\ge0$ always. When $q_\theta$ covers the support of $p$, $G_{\rm fwd}$ serves as an empirical estimator of $\mathrm{KL}(q_\theta\,\|\,p)$. However, when a basin is omitted, $G_{\rm fwd}$ reflects only the fit within the visited region $A$, approaching $\mathrm{KL}(q_A\,\|\,p_A)\approx0$ if $q_\theta$ fits $A$ well.

Conversely, because $\E_p[\ell]=\log Z+\mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,q_\theta)$, the reverse gap satisfies
\begin{equation}
\E_{p, q_\theta}[G_{\rm rev}] = \mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,q_\theta) + (\log Z - \E_{q_\theta}[\log\hat Z_M]).
\end{equation}
Both terms expand substantially when a basin of mass $\alpha$ is dropped: by data processing, $\mathrm{KL}(p\,\|\,q_\theta)\ge\alpha\log(\alpha/\epsilon)+(1-\alpha)\log\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\epsilon}$ with $\epsilon=q_\theta(B)\to0$, and $\log Z-\log\hat Z_M\approx-\log(1-\alpha)$. The forward gap sees neither. For a dropped basin of mass $\alpha=0.081$ with $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ (in the alanine surrogate we only know that no draw in $5\times10^5$ landed in $C_{7\rm ax}$, so $\epsilon\lesssim10^{-5}$), these two terms contribute about 0.9\,nats (with $1$\,nat $\approx 0.596$\,kcal/mol at 300\,K), while density mismatch elevates $G_{\rm rev}$ to $\valaGrev$\,nats. We do not propose a universal threshold: in Table~\ref{tab:3d_flows} a mixture with the correct support reaches $\valaThreeDTwinGrev$\,nats. This mirrors Bennett's acceptance ratio \citep{bennett1976bar} adapted for mode coverage.

\paragraph{Operational role: auditing amortized models.}
A common practical concern is whether requiring a reference sampler makes the learned sampler redundant: if an accurate reference exists, why train a neural proposal? The answer lies in amortization. In applications such as high-throughput screening or continuous conformational generation, learned samplers generate thousands of equilibrium configurations per second. Heavy classical samplers (such as replica exchange MD or parallel tempering) are too computationally expensive for high-throughput generation, but they can be executed offline on a modest validation set. By evaluating the reverse gap $G_{\rm rev}$ on these benchmark targets, practitioners can check whether the amortized network has dropped basins that the reference visits. Furthermore, the reference sampler does not need to be mathematically exact: as long as it visits the basin missed by $q_\theta$, the reverse gap grows with the omission; a large gap can also occur with the correct support (Table~\ref{tab:3d_flows}), so it calls for a closer look rather than proving a dropped basin. Standard multi-chain diagnostics (such as Gelman-Rubin $\hat{R} < 1.05$ or replica-exchange round-trip rates) can be used to ensure the offline reference set has visited the relevant basins before auditing $q_\theta$.

\paragraph{Exact toy validation.} The double well of Figure~\ref{fig:molecular_pathology} has
\begin{equation}
U(x)=-2.5\,e^{-(x+1.1)^2/(2\cdot0.18^2)}-1.2\,e^{-(x-1.1)^2/(2\cdot0.65^2)}+0.15\,x^2,\qquad\beta=1.2.
\end{equation}
By numerical quadrature, the two basins (divided at the barrier peak, $x=\vtoySplit$) have Boltzmann weights $\vtoyZone$ and $\vtoyZtwo$. The wider basin holds $\alpha=\vtoyMassTwo\%$ of the equilibrium mass despite having a higher local energy minimum, due to entropic width. The proposal mixes tempered densities of the two basins, placing mass $1-\epsilon$ on the narrow well and $\epsilon$ on the wide well. We draw $M=\vbdM$ proposals and $K=\vbdM$ exact reference draws in each of $\vbdRuns$ independent runs per value of $\epsilon$.

For $\epsilon\le10^{-4}$, almost no run proposes the wide basin (at $\epsilon=\vbdEps$, $\vbdNoDisc\%$ of runs). In these runs, the median ESS is $\vbdESS\%$ and the forward Jensen gap is nearly zero ($\vbdJensen$ nats), falsely signaling excellent convergence. Meanwhile, the true $\log Z$ error is $\vbdErr$ nats (matching the theoretical shift $-\log(1-\alpha)=\vbdTarget$), and nominal 95\% intervals cover the true free energy in $\vbdCov\%$ of runs (Figure~\ref{fig:bidirectional_solution}). In sharp contrast, the reverse gap detects the omission immediately, reporting $\vbdBidir$ nats.

\paragraph{A continuous flow on an alanine surrogate.}
To evaluate Proposition~\ref{prop:unvisited} and the reverse check on a continuous model, we trained a 4-layer RealNVP normalizing flow \citep{noe2019boltzmann} via reverse-KL divergence on an analytic two-dimensional surrogate of the alanine dipeptide Ramachandran surface $(\phi, \psi)$ at 300\,K: three Gaussian wells ($C_{7\rm eq}$, $\alpha_R$, $C_{7\rm ax}$) and a barrier at $\phi=0$. It is not a force field, and its basin masses are properties of the surrogate. By fine 2D quadrature ($300\times300$ grid), the metastable $C_{7\rm ax}$ basin ($\phi > 0$) accounts for $\valaAlpha\%$ of the equilibrium Boltzmann mass ($F_{\rm exact} = \valaFexact$\,kcal/mol). 

Under reverse-KL training, the flow collapses into the deeper $C_{7\rm eq}$ basin and proposes $\valaSamp\%$ of draws in $C_{7\rm ax}$. Despite completely omitting this state, across 100 independent evaluation runs ($M=5{,}000$ draws each), the flow reports a median internal ESS of $\valaESS\%$ and nominal 95\% confidence intervals covering the exact free energy in only $\valaCov\%$ of runs ($\pm\valaCI$\,kcal/mol interval width around a $+\valaErr$\,kcal/mol biased estimate). 

The forward Jensen gap reports $G_{\rm fwd} = \valaGfwd$\,nats, blind to the missing fold. However, the reverse check evaluated on independent reference draws immediately spikes to $G_{\rm rev} = \valaGrev$\,nats. Most of this gap comes from reference draws in $C_{7\rm ax}$, where the flow's density is tiny; we did not store the per-basin decomposition.

\paragraph{Higher-dimensional model landscapes.}
We repeated the audit with flows in 66 to 414 Cartesian dimensions on three landscapes whose $\log Z$ is known (Table~\ref{tab:3d_flows}, Figure~\ref{fig:3d_diagnostic_blindness}): two analytic surrogates, for which $\log Z$ is exact, and the real Lennard-Jones potential of LJ$_{38}$ restricted to two basins, for which it is estimated. None is a biomolecular force field, and none tests whether a published molecular sampler drops a basin.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Alanine surrogate in 3D (66D).} The analytic Ramachandran surface above plus harmonic bond, angle and stiff-torsion terms, mapped to $\mathbb{R}^{22\times3}$ through internal coordinates ($\valaThreeDParams$-parameter coupling flow). The flow trained by reverse KL samples $C_{7\rm ax}$ in fewer than 0.01\% of draws, reports a median ESS of $\valaThreeDESS\%$, and its intervals cover in $\valaThreeDCov\%$ of runs; $G_{\rm fwd}=\valaThreeDGfwd$ and $G_{\rm rev}=\valaThreeDGrev$\,nats. The control, a mixture of two flows with the exact basin weights, also fails: it covers in $\valaThreeDTwinCov\%$ of runs with error $\valaThreeDTwinErr$\,kcal/mol and $G_{\rm rev}=\valaThreeDTwinGrev$\,nats. Its error cannot come from a missing basin, since it samples $C_{7\rm ax}$ at the equilibrium rate; we did not identify its source. In this case the reverse gap does not separate the two proposals. The single flow's error ($\valaThreeDErr$\,kcal/mol) is also larger than the $\valaBias$\,kcal/mol a dropped basin alone explains.
\item \textbf{LJ$_{38}$ two-basin model (114D).} The standard Lennard-Jones potential of the cluster (it reproduces the stored minimum energies, $\vljEfcc$ and $\vljEico$) at $T=0.012$. Each basin's $\log Z$ is estimated by MALA started at its minimum and BAR against a harmonic reference (32 chains, $\hat R<1.012$; standard error 0.004). Other minima and permutational isomers are not included, so this is a two-basin approximation of the cluster's partition function. At this temperature the FCC basin holds almost all the mass. A RealNVP flow and an $SE(3)$-invariant distance flow, each confined to the icosahedral basin, report median ESS of $\vljThreeDESS\%$ and $\vljThreeDSEThreeESS\%$ with 0\% coverage, while $G_{\rm rev}$ is $\vljThreeDGrev$ and $\vljThreeDSEThreeGrev$\,nats. The Pareto $\hat k$ of the $SE(3)$ flow's weights is $\vljThreeDSEThreekhat$. Two-flow mixtures with fixed equal weights cover in $\vljThreeDTwinCov\%$ of runs.
\item \textbf{Two-Gaussian 408D model.} Two anisotropic Gaussian wells in the 408 internal Cartesian coordinates of a 138-atom chignolin structure, one at the experimental structure and one displaced in a random direction, with the offset set so that the first well holds 68\% of the mass. The energy is not Amber14SB, and the 68\% is an input taken from the literature value discussed in Section~\ref{sec:molecular_case}. A flow confined to the second well reports a median ESS of $\vchigThreeDESS\%$ and 0\% coverage, with error $\vchigThreeDErr$\,kcal/mol, equal to $-k_BT\log(1-0.68)$ as Proposition~\ref{prop:unvisited} predicts. $G_{\rm rev}$ is $\vchigThreeDGrev$\,nats, against $\vchigThreeDTwinGrev$\,nats for the two-flow mixture with the exact weights, which covers in $\vchigThreeDTwinCov\%$ of runs.
\end{enumerate}
The controls are mixtures of two separately trained flows with fixed weights, not single samplers that found both basins; they show what a proposal with the right support reports, not that such a proposal is easy to train. We computed the Pareto $\hat k$ from the flows' own weights only for the $SE(3)$ model; the values in the v2 preprint for the other flows were not computed that way and are withdrawn.
