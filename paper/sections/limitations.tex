\paragraph{Continuous 3D scale and architectures.}
In Section~\ref{sec:bidirectional}, we extended our empirical audit from 2D coordinates to continuous 3D Cartesian coordinates up to 414 dimensions, testing cross-coupled NeRF bijections, Eckart vibrational projections, and exact $SE(3)$-equivariant distance-message-passing continuous flows. While these evaluations demonstrate that diagnostic blindness is mathematically invariant to spatial dimension and geometric equivariance, scaling full all-atom neural Boltzmann generators to multi-hundred-residue proteins with explicit solvent remains an open computational frontier for the field. Continuous neural proposals mathematically possess full support on $\mathbb{R}^D$ through Gaussian tails; however, whenever free-energy barriers suppress transition probabilities to $\epsilon \ll 1/M$, finite samples behave identically to truncated support.

\paragraph{Architectural scope and training objectives.}
On the exact spin-glass benchmark, our neural evaluation focused on two iterative samplers (masked diffusion and GFlowNets) and one normalized autoregressive model (VAN-MADE), all trained via reverse-KL or variational objectives. These objectives are inherently mode-seeking; while forward-KL or hybrid objectives theoretically encourage mode covering, they require pre-existing equilibrium training data that autonomous samplers seek to avoid. Furthermore, while VAN-MADE's 1D autoregressive factorization penalizes mode omission, this mechanism does not straightforwardly transfer to continuous 3D molecular coordinates, where sequential orderings break SE(3) roto-translational equivariance.

\paragraph{Trajectories, equilibrium baselines, and kinetics.}
Our atomistic simulations used single unbiased 300\,ns Langevin trajectories to illustrate kinetic trapping. Because these trajectories do not equilibrate (chignolin departs the native fold without returning, and LJ$_{38}$ remains trapped in the FCC funnel), observed simulation fractions describe non-ergodic paths rather than true force-field equilibria. We therefore anchored all thermodynamic free-energy shift calculations to converged Anton multi-microsecond MD and experimental NMR literature baselines. Moreover, our analysis focuses strictly on static equilibrium free energies ($F=-\beta^{-1}\log Z$) rather than dynamical transition path times or kinetic rates ($k_{\rm on}, k_{\rm off}$).

\paragraph{Reference dependence, one-sided guarantees, and calibration.}
The reverse check $G_{\rm rev}$ provides a \emph{one-sided necessary condition} rather than an autonomous ergodicity solver. Because $G_{\rm rev}$ lower-bounds $\alpha\log(1/\epsilon)$, a large gap ($G_{\rm rev} \gg 0$) definitively signals mode omission or severe density mismatch. However, if both the neural proposal and the reference sampler fail to visit a cryptic basin (e.g.\ due to shared kinetic trapping across unconverged chains), both forward and reverse gaps remain blind. Evaluating $G_{\rm rev}$ across independent reference chains certified by convergence diagnostics (such as $\hat{R} < 1.05$ or NMR consensus) ensures observed gaps exceed Monte Carlo sampling noise.

\paragraph{Compute budgets and hardware normalization.}
The spin-glass benchmark equalizes total energy-evaluation budgets across methods ($10^5$ calls), incorporating neural training iterations into the cost. Wall-clock measurements reflect single-thread execution for reproducible comparison, but do not capture batched GPU parallelism during production inference where amortized neural samplers gain substantial throughput.
